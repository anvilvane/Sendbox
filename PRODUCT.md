# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Cold-email operators who need outreach to actually reach the inbox: agencies running outbound for multiple clients, SDR/GTM teams sending at volume, and recruiters reaching candidates who ignore InMail. They're evaluating cold email platforms and comparing Sendbox against Instantly, Smartlead, Lemlist, Apollo, Reply.io, Saleshandy, and Woodpecker. They've likely been burned by shared-IP deliverability problems before and are skeptical of feature-list marketing.

## Product Purpose

Sendbox is cold email software built on dedicated IPs and isolated sending infrastructure, with AI-powered warmup, per-mailbox health monitoring, and deliverability tooling most platforms sell as an add-on. It exists because teams often only discover their emails are landing in spam after a campaign has already failed. Success looks like a visitor trusting the deliverability claims enough to start a trial, specifically because the site itself reads as credible rather than as another cold-email SaaS marketing page.

## Brand Personality

Bold, direct, no-BS. Sharp and confident rather than corporate-safe; willing to concede a competitor's real strength rather than oversell. The site's own honesty (public changelog, no hidden per-seat pricing, admitted gaps) is a personality trait, not just a footnote.

## Anti-references

This codebase started as a rebranded clone of a real competitor (SendKit.ai) — same layout, same section structure, same component patterns, just recolored. That is the explicit thing to move away from: a marketing site that reads as "reskinned SaaS template" rather than a considered design for this brand. Not a full from-scratch rebuild — targeted redesign of the sections that read most like a generic template, working within the existing codebase and component system rather than starting over.

## Design Principles

- Redesign in place, section by section — the existing dark-violet-on-white brand color, Poppins/Figtree/Inter type system, and overall page structure stay; the goal is to stop specific sections from reading as a template clone, not to relaunch the brand.
- Concede competitor strengths in comparison content; confidence reads as more credible than a page that only ever wins.
- Avoid the current era's AI-marketing-page tells (uniform eyebrow labels on every section, identical card grids, hero-metric template, gradient text) — vary the pattern per section instead of repeating one scaffold site-wide.
- Prioritize visible, above-the-fold sections first (Hero, Why/Platform, pricing, comparison content) over deep interior pages.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Standard WCAG 2.1 AA: body text ≥4.5:1 contrast, visible focus states, reduced-motion alternative for every animation. No additional stated requirements beyond that baseline.
